# Content and Route Quality Guidelines

Route quality is the moat. Bad routes destroy trust.

## Every route needs

- Clear title
- Honest summary
- Accurate distance
- Estimated duration
- Valid geometry
- Start/end point
- Route type
- Tags/interests
- Stop list
- At least one “why this route” reason
- At least one practical note
- Last reviewed date
- Source quality status

## Good route titles

Good:

- Old Montreal Stone Streets Loop
- Mount Royal Sunrise Viewpoint Walk
- Plateau Murals & Café Walk
- Lachine Canal Waterfront Loop
- Downtown Churches & Architecture Route

Bad:

- Nice Walk
- Best Route
- Montreal Route 1
- Easy Walk Around Town

## Route copy style

Use plain, practical language:

- what the user will see
- why it is worth doing
- what makes it different
- what to know before going

Avoid generic filler:

- “Embark on an unforgettable journey”
- “Discover hidden gems and vibrant culture” with no specifics
- “Perfect for everyone”

## Field-testing status

Each route should have one of:

- draft
- reviewed from map/data
- field tested
- needs update

## Practical note examples

- Cobblestones in Old Montreal
- Steep sections on Mount Royal
- Better in daylight
- Busy on weekends
- Public bathrooms nearby
- Ends near metro station
- Good winter option / not good winter option

## Route scoring inputs

- distance
- estimated time
- stop density
- scenery score
- culture/history score
- café/food score
- quietness score
- hill/stair burden
- transit access
- loop simplicity
- first-time visitor fit

## Do not publish routes that

- have broken geometry
- run through private land
- have unsafe crossings if avoidable
- include irrelevant filler stops
- are technically valid but boring
- use misleading “easy” labels
- have no practical notes
